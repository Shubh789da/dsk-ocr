CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY:
29
30
Tretinoin is an endogenous retinoid metabolite of Vitamin A that binds to
31
intracellular receptors in the cytosol and nucleus, but cutaneous levels of tretinoin in
32
excess of physiologic concentrations occur following application of a tretinoin
33
containing topical drug product.   Although tretinoin activates three members of the
34
retinoic acid (RAR) nuclear receptors (RARα, RARβ, and RARγ) which may act to
35
modify gene expression, subsequent protein synthesis, and epithelial cell growth
36
and differentiation, it has not been established whether the clinical effects of
37
tretinoin are mediated through activation of retinoic acid receptors, other
38
mechanisms such as irritation, or both.

2
The effect of tretinoin on skin with chronic photodamage has not been evaluated in
1
animal studies. When hairless albino mice were treated topically with tretinoin
2
shortly after a period of UVB irradiation, new collagen formation was demonstrated
3
only in photodamaged skin. However, in human skin treated topically, adequate
4
data have not been provided to demonstrate any increase in desmosine,
5
hydroxyproline, or elastin mRNA.  Application of 0.1% tretinoin cream to
6
photodamaged human forearm skin  was associated with an increase in antibody-
7
staining for procollagen I propeptide.  No correlation was made between
8
procollagen I propeptide staining with collagen I levels or with observed clinical
9
effects.  Thus, the relationships between the increased collagen in rodents,
10
increased procollagen I propeptide in humans, and the clinical effects of  tretinoin
11
have not yet been clearly defined.
12
13
Tretinoin was shown to enhance UV stimulated melanogenesis in pigmented mice.
14
Generalized amyloid deposition in the basal layer of tretinoin treated skin was noted
15
in a two year mouse study.  In a different study, hyalinization at tretinoin treated skin
16
sites was noted at doses beginning at 0.25 mg/kg in CD-1 mice.
17
18
The transdermal absorption of tretinoin from various topical formulations ranged
19
from 1% to 31% of applied dose, depending on whether it was applied to healthy
20
skin or dermatitic skin.  No percutaneous absorption study was conducted with
21
RENOVA 0.02% in human volunteers.  When percutaneous absorption of the oil-in-
22
water emulsion formulation at 0.05% concentration was assessed in healthy male
23
subjects with radiolabeled cream after a single application (n=7), as well as after
24

25
than 2% and the extent of bioavailability was less after repeated application.  No
26
significant difference in endogenous concentrations of tretinoin was observed

between single and repeated daily applications.

Teratogenic effects:  Pregnancy Category C.
2
3
4
ORAL tretinoin has been shown to be teratogenic in rats, mice, rabbits, hamsters,
5
and subhuman primates.  It was teratogenic and fetotoxic in Wistar rats when given
6
orally or topically in doses greater than 1 mg/kg/day (42 times the maximum human
7
systemic dose normalized for total body surface area).  However, variations in
8
teratogenic doses among various strains of rats have been reported.  In the
9
cynomolgus monkey, which, metabolically, is closer to humans for tretinoin than the
10
other species examined, fetal malformations were reported at doses of 10
11
mg/kg/day or greater, but none were observed at 5 mg/kg/day (417 times the
12
maximum human systemicdose adjusted for total body surface area), although
13
increased skeletal variations were observed at all doses.  A dose-related increase
14
in embryolethality and abortion was reported.  Similar results have also been
15
reported in pigtail macaques.
16
17
TOPICAL tretinoin in animal teratogenicity tests has generated equivocal results. 
18
There is evidence for teratogenicity (shortened or kinked tail) of topical tretinoin in
19
Wistar rats at doses greater than 1 mg/kg/day (42 times the maximum human 
20
systemic dose adjusted for total body surface area).  Anomalies (humerus: short
21
13%, bent 6%, os parietal incompletely ossified 14%) have also been reported
22
when 10 mg/kg/day was dermally applied.
23
24
There are other reports in New Zealand White rabbits administered doses of
25
greater than 0.2 mg/kg/day (17 times the maximum human systemic dose adjusted
26
for total body surface area) of an increased incidence of domed head and
27
hydrocephaly, typical of retinoid-induced fetal malformations in this species.
28
29
In contrast, several well-controlled animal studies have shown that dermally applied
30
tretinoin may be fetotoxic, but not overtly teratogenic, in rats and rabbits at doses of
31
1.0 and 0.5 mg/kg/day, respectively (42 times the maximum human systemic dose
32
adjusted for total body surface area in both species).
33
34
With widespread use of any drug, a small number of birth defect reports associated
35
temporally with the administration of the drug would be expected by chance alone.

Thirty human cases of temporally-associated congenital malformations have been

reported during two decades of clinical use of another formulation of topical tretinoin

(Retin-A).  Although no definite pattern of teratogenicity and no causal association

has been established from these cases, 5 of the reports describe the rare birth

defect category holoprosencephaly (defects associated with incomplete midline

development of the forebrain).  The significance of these spontaneous reports in

What is RENOVA?
23
RENOVA 0.02% is a prescription medicine that may reduce fine facial wrinkles.  It
24
is for patients who are using a total skin care and sunlight avoidance program.
25
RENOVA does not remove wrinkles or repair sun-damaged skin. RENOVA does
26
not work for everyone who uses it.  It may work better for some patients than for
27
others.
28
29
RENOVA should be used only under the guidance of your doctor as part of a
30
sunlight avoidance program and total skin care.  This program should include
31
avoiding sunlight as much as possible, using clothing to protect you from sunlight,
32
using sunscreens with a minimum SPF of 15, and using face creams that add
33
moisture to the skin.
34
35
When you use RENOVA, you will not see improvement right away. Generally, you
36
may notice some effects in 3 to 4 months. If RENOVA treatment is stopped, the
37
improvement may gradually disappear.
38
39
The use of RENOVA 0.02% in patients for more than 52 weeks has not been
40
studied.  Therefore, it is not known if RENOVA 0.02% is safe or works if used
41
longer than 52 weeks.  In a study in people with medium to dark skin color,
42
RENOVA 0.02% has not demonstrated a benefit over a sunlight avoidance program
43
and total skin care.  RENOVA 0.02% has not been studied in people with visible
44
actinic keratosis, or in people with a history of skin cancer.

28

29

30

31

35

36

39
